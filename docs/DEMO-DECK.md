# Official Demo Deck — "General Knowledge Showcase"

The first deck to ship with Trivia Night. Used for MVP testing, friends & family
playtests, university demos, investor demos, and UX testing. Broad general
knowledge, no niche specialist topics, no current events, no politics or
religion — safe for any group of friends, family, or students to pick up cold.

**Format:** 4 Rounds × 5 Questions = 20 Questions
**Types:** 12 Multiple Choice, 8 Typed Answer (mixed throughout, not clustered)
**Estimated playtime:** 20–30 minutes
**Difficulty curve:** Easy → Easy/Medium → Medium/Hard → Hard (gradual, fair, rewards thinking over obscure trivia)

Copy each question directly into the Deck Editor in order. Explanations are
written for the Host to read aloud after the reveal.

---

## Round 1 — Warm-Up
*Purpose: build confidence. Everyone should score points here.*

### Q1 · Multiple Choice · Easy
**Question:** How many continents are there on Earth?
- A) 5
- B) 6
- C) 7
- D) 8

**Correct Answer:** C) 7
**Explanation:** Antarctica counts too, even though nobody lives there permanently except researchers brave enough to handle the cold for science.

---

### Q2 · Typed Answer · Easy
**Question:** What is the largest planet in our solar system?
**Correct Answer:** Jupiter
**Explanation:** Jupiter is so big that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. Its famous Great Red Spot is a storm that's been raging for at least 350 years.

---

### Q3 · Multiple Choice · Easy
**Question:** Which of these is the largest species of big cat?
- A) Lion
- B) Tiger
- C) Jaguar
- D) Leopard

**Correct Answer:** B) Tiger
**Explanation:** Tigers can weigh over 300kg, and the Siberian tiger is the biggest subspecies — built for hunting through freezing Russian winters.

---

### Q4 · Multiple Choice · Easy
**Question:** What is the capital city of France?
- A) Marseille
- B) Lyon
- C) Paris
- D) Nice

**Correct Answer:** C) Paris
**Explanation:** Paris has been France's capital for over a thousand years, though the government did briefly relocate south during the Second World War.

---

### Q5 · Typed Answer · Easy
**Question:** What food do bees produce that people eat?
**Correct Answer:** Honey
**Explanation:** A single bee only makes about a twelfth of a teaspoon of honey in its entire lifetime — so that jar in your cupboard represents a lot of teamwork.

---

## Round 2 — Entertainment & Pop Culture
*Purpose: recognisable, fun, slightly harder. One question each from movies, music, gaming, TV, and internet culture.*

### Q6 · Multiple Choice · Easy
**Question:** Which fictional wizard has a lightning-bolt scar on his forehead?
- A) Gandalf
- B) Merlin
- C) Harry Potter
- D) Dumbledore

**Correct Answer:** C) Harry Potter
**Explanation:** The scar was left by Voldemort's failed curse — and it doubles as one of the most recognisable pieces of movie makeup ever created.

---

### Q7 · Typed Answer · Medium
**Question:** Which British rock band released the album "Abbey Road"?
**Accepted Answers:** The Beatles, Beatles
**Explanation:** The iconic cover photo of the band crossing the street was shot in just ten minutes, with the photographer balanced on a stepladder in the middle of the road.

---

### Q8 · Multiple Choice · Medium
**Question:** Which video game franchise features a plumber named Mario?
- A) Sonic
- B) Zelda
- C) Kirby
- D) Super Mario

**Correct Answer:** D) Super Mario
**Explanation:** Mario actually started out as "Jumpman" in the 1981 arcade game Donkey Kong, before he got his own name, red hat, and mustache.

---

### Q9 · Multiple Choice · Medium
**Question:** Which streaming service produced the show "Stranger Things"?
- A) Hulu
- B) Disney+
- C) Netflix
- D) Amazon Prime

**Correct Answer:** C) Netflix
**Explanation:** Stranger Things was reportedly turned down by around 15–20 other networks before Netflix picked it up and turned it into one of its biggest hits.

---

### Q10 · Typed Answer · Medium
**Question:** What internet term describes an image, video, or phrase that spreads rapidly online?
**Accepted Answers:** Meme, Internet meme
**Explanation:** The word actually predates the internet by decades — biologist Richard Dawkins coined it in 1976 to describe how ideas spread and evolve like genes.

---

## Round 3 — Challenge Round
*Purpose: raise the difficulty, mix formats, spark debate before the reveal.*

### Q11 · Multiple Choice · Medium
**Question:** Which country has the most natural lakes in the world?
- A) Russia
- B) Finland
- C) Canada
- D) USA

**Correct Answer:** C) Canada
**Explanation:** Canada holds roughly 60% of the world's lakes — over 2 million of them — thanks to ancient glaciers carving out the landscape. Most don't even have names.

---

### Q12 · Typed Answer · Medium
**Question:** What is the only mammal capable of true, sustained flight?
**Correct Answer:** Bat
**Explanation:** Flying squirrels only glide, but bats truly fly — their wings are actually made from elongated finger bones stretched under a thin membrane of skin.

---

### Q13 · Multiple Choice · Hard
**Question:** Which artist painted "The Starry Night"?
- A) Claude Monet
- B) Pablo Picasso
- C) Salvador Dalí
- D) Vincent van Gogh

**Correct Answer:** D) Vincent van Gogh
**Explanation:** He painted it from memory while staying in an asylum in France, looking out at the view from his window. He sold only one painting during his entire lifetime.

---

### Q14 · Typed Answer · Hard
**Question:** What is the term for an intense fear of spiders?
**Accepted Answers:** Arachnophobia
**Explanation:** "Arachno" comes from the Greek word for spider — and spiders technically aren't even insects. They're arachnids, in the same family as scorpions and mites.

---

### Q15 · Multiple Choice · Hard
**Question:** Which element has the chemical symbol "Au"?
- A) Silver
- B) Aluminum
- C) Gold
- D) Argon

**Correct Answer:** C) Gold
**Explanation:** "Au" comes from the Latin word "aurum," meaning "shining dawn" — a fitting name for one of history's most sought-after metals.

---

## Round 4 — Final Challenge
*Purpose: hardest round, still fair. Reward thinking over obscure trivia. Finish strong.*

### Q16 · Multiple Choice · Hard
**Question:** Which ancient wonder of the world is the only one still standing today?
- A) Hanging Gardens of Babylon
- B) Colossus of Rhodes
- C) Great Pyramid of Giza
- D) Lighthouse of Alexandria

**Correct Answer:** C) Great Pyramid of Giza
**Explanation:** It's already over 4,500 years old, and it held the title of tallest man-made structure on Earth for almost 3,800 years — until the Eiffel Tower finally beat it in 1889.

---

### Q17 · Typed Answer · Hard
**Question:** What is the term for a word or phrase that reads the same forwards and backwards?
**Accepted Answers:** Palindrome
**Explanation:** "Racecar" and "level" are classic examples — some people have even written entire palindromic sentences, like "A man, a plan, a canal, Panama."

---

### Q18 · Multiple Choice · Hard
**Question:** What is the hardest known natural substance on Earth?
- A) Titanium
- B) Quartz
- C) Steel
- D) Diamond

**Correct Answer:** D) Diamond
**Explanation:** Diamonds are so hard because their carbon atoms are locked into an incredibly tight, rigid structure — the exact same element that makes up soft pencil graphite, just arranged completely differently.

---

### Q19 · Typed Answer · Hard
**Question:** What is the largest organ in the human body?
**Accepted Answers:** Skin
**Explanation:** An adult's skin covers about 2 square metres and makes up roughly 15% of total body weight — more than the brain and liver combined.

---

### Q20 · Multiple Choice · Hard (Final Question)
**Question:** Which of these four inventions came first?
- A) The telephone
- B) The lightbulb
- C) Photography
- D) The zipper

**Correct Answer:** C) Photography
**Explanation:** The first permanent photograph was captured in 1826–27 — decades before the lightbulb (1879), the telephone (1876), or the zipper (1893). We tend to picture the pace of invention completely backwards.

---

## Quality Review Notes

- **No duplicate topics:** every question covers a distinct subject (geography, astronomy, animals, food, film, music, gaming, TV, internet culture, lakes, biology, art, language, chemistry, history, wordplay, materials science, human body, invention history).
- **Balanced types:** exactly 12 Multiple Choice / 8 Typed Answer, distributed 3 MC + 2 Typed per round so no round clusters one type.
- **Balanced difficulty:** 6 Easy → 6 Medium → 8 Hard, ramping gradually round over round; Round 1 is all Easy by design.
- **No ambiguous answers:** avoided debates with multiple "technically correct" answers (e.g., no Nile-vs-Amazon river questions); every Typed Answer has a short, common, easily-spelled correct answer.
- **No current events, politics, religion, or niche fandoms.**
- **Pacing:** confidence-building open → recognisable pop-culture fun → discussion-sparking mid-game debates (lakes, bats, van Gogh) → hardest round finishing on a genuine "I didn't see that coming" reveal.
